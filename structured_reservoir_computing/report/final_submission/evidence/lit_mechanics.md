### From rotational kinematics to a predictive feature map

A gear pair generates a periodic excitation through changes in contact stiffness and transmission error as successive teeth enter and leave engagement. In a controlled laboratory setting, an isolated tooth crack can reduce the local stiffness and change both amplitude and phase of the meshing response when the damaged tooth re-enters contact. Analytical cracked-tooth stiffness models give a mechanical basis for this mechanism, and McFadden's measurements demonstrated the diagnostic relevance of demodulating the mesh response. The casing accelerometer nevertheless measures a response after transmission through shafts, bearing supports and the structure. Both loading and measurement position can affect the observed pulse shape. The mechanism motivates a fault-order hypothesis without fixing the response amplitude, phase or shape for an individual record. [@chaari2009stiffness; @mcfadden1986gears; @chen2025sparse]

Shaft speed determines the physical frequency through cumulative revolutions. For measured input speed $s(t)$ in $\mathrm{rev\,min^{-1}}$, define $q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}s(u)/60\,du$. A component tied to $m$ events per input-shaft revolution has phase $2\pi m q(t)$ and instantaneous frequency $m s(t)/60$ Hz. In the cited Pretoria rig the test stage is a single-pair step-up helical gearbox with a 37-tooth input gear and a 20-tooth output pinion. Enforcing equal tooth passage rates gives a mesh order of 37 relative to the input and a mean output-to-input rotational frequency ratio of $37/20$. A defective tooth on the input gear can recur at input-shaft order one while perturbing the mesh carrier. Amplitude modulation by order $p$ produces sidebands at $m-p$ and $m+p$ in the simplest sinusoidal model. Assembly and load modulation can also produce sidebands, so their presence supplies a mechanism to investigate and has limited specificity for tooth damage. [@chen2025sparse; @mcfadden1986gears; @capdessus2000cyclo]

Constant-rate time samples see the carrier sweep through a range of frequencies as speed varies. Computed order tracking estimates a monotonically increasing angular trajectory and interpolates the measured vibration onto equally spaced angular samples. Shaft-synchronous content then maintains a fixed order, while a component with approximately fixed frequency $f_0$ appears at varying instantaneous order $f_0/(s/60)$. These are different physical hypotheses. A gearbox acceleration signal can sometimes support angular resampling in the absence of a tachometer under limited fluctuations, but the local study has a measured speed channel and uses that channel explicitly. A fixed-frequency resonance can overlap a moving order over part of a sweep. Assignment of a single time-averaged peak to a mode without checking speed dependence would therefore be ambiguous. Welch estimates provide interval-averaged power and a useful overview of broad energetic bands. A time-frequency ridge and an order-domain comparison supply the phase-locking evidence that a time-averaged spectrum alone leaves unresolved. [@fyfe1997order; @bonnardot2005resample; @abboud2017envelope; @welch1967fft]

Order tracking also imposes a metrological requirement. Errors in the speed channel accumulate into angular error, and the phase displacement of order $m$ is $2\pi m\,\Delta q$. Higher orders are consequently more sensitive to the same angular error. Interpolation can distort an envelope during speed variation, and the chosen angular grid sets the numerical representation, subject to the bandwidth already available from time sampling. At 3.2 kHz, the highest observed training speed limits the time-domain Nyquist bound to approximately order 574 even when interpolation produces 2,048 samples per revolution. The training speed range of 124.323–167.334 rev/min implies an input mesh band of 76.666–103.189 Hz. The order-148 ridge tracked its expected frequency with median absolute error of 0.744 Hz and a correlation of 0.987. The training order-148 peak was about 160 times its local median. These results support including speed-synchronous states. Identification of a structural resonance and certification of sensor accuracy require additional evidence. The observed 31.3% of Welch power in 1.0–1.2 kHz motivated a fixed-Hz candidate, whose 1,100 Hz addition gave 0.46230 guarded screening MSE versus 0.46153 for the common LPV-AR24 control. The order-148/259 quadrature addition gave 0.43803 on that screen. The final four-order bank was chosen separately in the guarded PI-SRC search. [@fyfe1997order; @cheng2014deformation; @abboud2017envelope]

### Operating-condition-dependent prediction and adjacent methods

A linear parameter-varying autoregression is linear in past samples for a fixed schedule and allows that coefficient vector to change with measured speed. Chen, Liang and Zuo used sparse functional pooled autoregression to represent healthy gearbox vibration with a speed-indexed basis. Earlier and later related work demonstrate that the operating trajectory and phase matter in diagnostic models. Chen et al. used speed and phase information to model impulse-rich vibration under random speed variation. Chen and Zuo extended sparse LPV autoregression to multichannel vibration, while Schmidt et al. proposed a probabilistic novelty method for gearboxes under fluctuating conditions. These methods establish precedent for conditioning on operating variables and for judging departures from baseline after accounting for operational variation. The local contribution is the controlled evaluation of a different feature map and its fault-sensitive selection, within a single-channel, strictly observed-history protocol. [@chen2019baseline; @chen2021crack; @chen2022multiv; @schmidt2018novelty]

The course's sparse LPV-ARMA comparator extends the schedule-dependent AR component by including delayed innovation proxies. The paper forms a product expansion of the lagged signal and innovation vectors with basis functions of the speed schedule. A sparse LPV-AR fit first yields residual proxies. A second stage combines those proxies with vibration lags, uses the LASSO and five-fold tuning to select support, and refits retained terms by least squares. The paper's penalty rule uses the largest penalty within one standard deviation of the minimum mean validation error. The generic rationale is support control in an expanded regression whose columns can be correlated. The local strict comparator restores the original protected sparse supports with a fresh refit and uses speed no later than $t-1$. PI-SRC instead uses a structured library containing explicit time and angle lags, finite quadrature memories, speed and energy interactions and separately controlled innovation and correction components. This distinction concerns the model construction and information set, even though both readouts regress on engineered histories. [@chen2025sparse]

### Residual processing, modulation specificity and speed-sensor interference

Removing correctly predicted baseline vibration can expose a weak repeated component, but a predictor can also absorb part of a fault signature because the measured history contains preceding fault responses. Envelope analysis addresses a different property from ordinary residual energy. For an angular trace $r_j$, the analytic magnitude $\left|r_j+i\mathcal H[r]_j\right|$ highlights modulation, which is then examined at the hypothesized recurrence order. In the rotating-machine literature, the envelope and the squared envelope spectrum are derived statistics linked to cyclic carrier modulation and frequency-band selection. Gear fault work demonstrates that such modulation can be diagnostic when the interfering mechanisms and measurement conditions are characterized. Zhao and Jia used PMI to select informative components for reweighted SVD, and Chen et al. applied the idea to gear fault extraction. In this project, the input to PMI is the measured-minus-forecast residual. [@randall2001envelope; @antoni2009cyclo; @abboud2017envelope; @zhao2017pmi; @chen2020svd; @chen2020vibration]

The course-protocol score uses an offline trapezoidal integral of rpm divided by 60 followed by linear interpolation of each retained trace onto a grid of 2,048 points per input-shaft revolution. Every candidate uses the same starting sample, zero-based 1,792, and the same diagnostic transform. The analytic magnitude in the lecturer-compatible branch is formed from the demeaned angular trace, with its mean then added back in accordance with the envelope convention. Centering the envelope before computing lag products cancels that additive mean. Let $a_j$ be this centered envelope and $N_\theta$ its length. The lag cross-product is $C_\ell=\sum_{j=0}^{N_\theta-1-\ell} a_j a_{j+\ell}$, with lag-zero energy $C_0=\sum_{j=0}^{N_\theta-1}a_j^2$. The score is $C_\ell/(C_0-C_\ell)$. This is equivalently $\rho_\ell/(1-\rho_\ell)$ for $\rho_\ell=C_\ell/C_0$, so PMI is dimensionless and monotonic in correlation wherever the denominator is positive. The mathematical one-turn setting uses $\ell=S=2048$. The literal MATLAB main branch uses the entry at one-based array index $S$, where the first autocorrelation entry is lag zero, corresponding to $\ell=S-1=2047$. The Python audit retains a separate exact-lag variant and a direct Hilbert-magnitude variant. Short traces and degenerate denominators are reported explicitly. The supplied script calls an external angular-resampling helper absent from the archived project inputs. Its literal convention is documented, with exact MATLAB numerical parity unverified. The local raw PMI is also distinct from any externally standardized healthy-reference scale. [@chen2020vibration; @chen2025sparse]

The paper interprets the ratio as repeated pulse energy relative to other envelope energy under assumptions about pulse periodicity and disturbance correlation. In this finite-record calculation, nonzero lag has fewer contributing sample pairs than lag zero. Other periodic mechanisms can create a positive cross-product, and finite-record sampling can leave a nonzero estimate in the absence of the specified fault. The ratio also becomes ill-conditioned as $\rho_\ell$ approaches one. A retained literal-vs-exact lag check therefore tests indexing and finite-record behavior independently of the interpretation of the real records. Observed PMI is evidence of repetition at the chosen lag. Attributing it to a particular crack requires independent labels or supporting diagnostic evidence. [@chen2020vibration; @chen2025sparse; @antoni2009cyclo; @abboud2017envelope]

The local candidate cohort exemplifies this distinction. Of the 200 supplied candidates, the project excluded one-based records 61 and 63 because their aligned initial vibration and RPM arrays reproduce the training and development measurements, respectively. Among the remaining index-defined groups of 98 and 100, the selected model's median lecturer-compatible PMI rose from 0.030189 in group A to 0.114919 in group B, a gap of 0.084730. The sparse LPV-ARMA and archived corrected SRC residuals gave gaps of 0.072552 and 0.103347, respectively. The paired acquisition-ID bootstrap interval for the selected-minus-sparse gap was $[0.008525,0.015952]$. The selected-minus-archived gap was $-0.018617$, with interval $[-0.021232,-0.016060]$. Several residual scores reached AUC 1.000 on this same unverified partition. The archival AUC tie coexists with a larger archive median gap. The gap and rank results characterize conditional separation, with no verified mapping of these records to health state or protection against dependence between different candidate files.

The frozen pulse intervention provides a separate waveform-specific measure. For a deliberately injected pulse $d$, define extra prediction $\Delta\widehat{x}=\widehat{x}(x+d)-\widehat{x}(x)$ using the same speed intervention in both runs. The residual response is $d-\Delta\widehat{x}$. Its energy ratio, correlation and projection quantify changes in waveform behavior, and the paired difference in PMI measures the effect on the modulation score. The evaluation used five phase-locked damped sine cases and two phase-randomized controls. The selected model increased PMI in five of five periodic cases and in zero of two aperiodic cases. Its median periodic PMI increase was 0.000422, while the median residual/pulse correlation was 0.690 and the energy ratio 1.79. The ratio above unity illustrates amplification or reshaping, and the small case count limits inference beyond the tested interventions. These are sensitivity checks on the existing development recording with known perturbations.

A comparable change in operating-condition measurement can have a much larger effect than the tested pulses. Applying a +2% RPM bias only to the predictor increased selected-model development MSE from 0.329973 to 0.457031 and increased PMI by 0.098194, despite leaving the diagnostic angular conversion on the original speed channel. Applying the same bias to the diagnostic angle computation alone changed PMI by 0.004508 with exactly the same prediction. Applying it in both places yielded a PMI increase of 0.044913. The observed difference among paths separates model-induced changes in the residual from the effect of a biased angular map. It also shows why mechanically meaningful speed information introduces a measurement requirement, particularly for higher orders. An instrumented, independently labelled assessment with varied loads, crack severities and calibrated speed measurements would be necessary to set an operating threshold and estimate its false-alarm behavior. [@fyfe1997order; @cheng2014deformation; @abboud2017envelope; @schmidt2018novelty]

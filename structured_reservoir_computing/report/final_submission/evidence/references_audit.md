# Literature search and attribution record

The report uses a targeted scholarly search for methods implemented in the SRC release and nearby scientific work that tests or qualifies the stated novelty. The search examined publisher issue pages, DOI/Crossref metadata, recognized proceedings or preprint archives for conference records, and the supplied journal article and doctoral thesis. Publications were retained when they explain a concrete design, establish a comparator’s provenance, or constrain a conclusion that the local data alone cannot establish. Experimental values from another study retain the original experiment and measurement scope. Search records and claim-level evidence are provided in the three specialist audits.

Searches were run for the above concept groups and followed by primary-source checks. The bibliography contains 59 unique cited entries, including 55 with DOI identifiers. A local course handout and three method records use supplied or persistent-source attribution where a DOI is unavailable or the adopted edition uses an alternate identifier. The final citeproc build resolves each cited key once. The build checks duplicate DOI values, unreferenced keys and the generated reference count. The institutional repository’s thesis DOI and the article’s publication year, volume and article number were matched to the original supplied texts.

The works most directly bearing on methodological originality are Gauthier et al. on explicit delay/polynomial readouts, Adler et al. on physics-informed nonlinear autoregression, Chen et al. on operating-condition scheduling, and Fyfe and Munck on computed order tracking. The direct source URLs, exact titles and associated limitations are in the individual audits. The research manuscript uses these sources to distinguish established mathematical components from the project’s gearbox-specific construction and empirical comparison.

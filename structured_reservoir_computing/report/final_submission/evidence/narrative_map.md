# Research narrative map

The report follows the decision path from physical observation to an evaluated predictor and bounded diagnostic claims. Chapter and appendix references match the final manuscript. [Manuscript](../report_source.md)

| Stage | Report location | Argument and decision evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineering phenomenon | §§1–3, Figure 3.1 | As speed varies, temporal mesh frequency moves while input-shaft order stays fixed. A tooth-related perturbation may recur near the same shaft angle and appear in the vibration envelope. The measured input-gear geometry and the recorded vibration/RPM channels define the forecast and diagnostic tasks. |
| Scientific gap | §§2 and 7 | Fixed-coefficient autoregression, speed-scheduled sparse models, generic nonlinear regressors and learned sequence encoders represent history differently. The experiment asks whether a gearbox-specific feature map gives a better measured trade-off under a shared one-step information cutoff. Prior work motivates order tracking, explicit delay maps and physics-informed feature design. |
| Training-only physical evidence | §4, Figures 4.1–4.2 and Table 4.1 | The order-148 ridge tracks the predicted mesh harmonic, and angular resampling concentrates that order. Motion of the mesh period motivates both time and angular history. Synchronous channels and selected sidebands help in training screens. Fixed-Hz candidates add little in those tests, and the selected fit uses shaft-synchronous carriers. |
| Implemented hypothesis | §5, Figure 5.1 and Table 5.1 | Derive the strict lag-one state, finite angular delays and damped quadratures. Combine them with lagged proxy innovations, operating context and screened nonlinear interactions. Fit a group-weighted ridge readout on clean and protection rows, with a separately controlled finite correction. The selected checkpoint encodes 879 columns and 1,456 fitted coefficients. |
| Selection and freeze | §6, Figure 6.1 and Tables 6.1–6.2 | Screen 41 configurations on three guarded training blocks. Choose the eligible configuration that minimizes clean squared error plus weighted stress, pulse and size terms. Freeze before development scoring. Retain the minimum-clean-error finalist and earlier-only sensitivity so the scope of the chosen utility is visible. |
| Matched comparisons | §§7–8, Tables 8.1–8.4 and Appendices A–B | Compare 21 fitted families and five archive rows with the primary on the same 22,208 targets. Disclose unequal fit budgets, measured-speed controls, seed sensitivity and within-record paired uncertainty. Compare CPU cost under the recorded one-thread batch protocol. Attribute journal and local current-RPM values to their own segments and timing conventions. |
| Diagnostic controls | §9, Figures 9.1–9.3 and Tables 9.1–9.2 | Apply a common angular envelope and PMI protocol to the 198 nonoverlapping candidate records. Treat A/B as provisional index groups. Probe preservation with periodic injections and aperiodic controls, then isolate RPM effects in the predictor, angle tracker and joint path. |
| Attribution and conclusion | §§10–11, Figures 10.1–10.2, Appendix C | Use feature ablations and fixed-weight correction controls to interpret the gain. Retain adverse comparisons and connect the next experiment to independently labelled acquisitions, calibrated speed and measured deployment latency. |

## Decision evidence for H1–H4

- **H1, matched prediction.** PI-SRC development MSE is 0.329973, improving 10.18% on Linear Regression and 11.16% on strict sparse LPV–ARMA on the shared targets. The corrected archive scores 0.317788. The primary's training MSE is 0.308688. [Matched results](../assets/tables/all_model_comparison.csv) · [Primary metrics](../assets/tables/src_summary.json)
- **H2, physical representation and correction.** Removing angle history raises development MSE to 0.359049. With the same base weights, activating the correction lowers MSE from 0.340706 to 0.329973. The structural and fixed-weight interventions carry different attribution scope. [Structural ablations](../assets/tables/src_ablations.csv) · [Fixed-weight controls](../assets/tables/identical_adapter_controls.csv)
- **H3, periodic residual evidence.** The primary median PMI group gap is 0.08473. All five periodic injections increase PMI, while predictor-only +2% RPM bias increases it by 0.09819. Confirmed conditions and fault decision thresholds require independently labelled measurements. [Group summary](../assets/tables/fault_group_summary.csv) · [Pulse results](../assets/tables/pulse_summary.csv) · [RPM interventions](../assets/tables/rpm_stress.csv)
- **H4, efficiency and robustness.** The primary warm CPU median is 484.89 ms for a 7.5 s record. Both strict sparse LPV–ARMA and the corrected archive are faster. The corrected archive also scores lower on the clean segment. Independent prediction, GPU timing and measured streaming latency remain open.
